# 90-Second Intro — Pransh Agarwal
## AI Consultant (AI-Native New Business Development), I’mbesideyou Inc.

Speak this at a calm, conversational pace. It is about **220 words** (~85–95 seconds).
Do not recite it like a script. Hit the beats. Keep the numbers. Smile on the close.

---

## What to say

Good morning. I’m Pransh Agarwal. I’m in my first semester of the MBA in Business Analytics at IIM Sambalpur.

I grew up in a close-knit nuclear family, and that upbringing taught me responsibility very early — you don’t wait to be told; you see the gap, you own it, you close it.

I completed my Bachelor’s in Computer Science from Chitkara University with a 9.34 CGPA. That CS foundation is why I can sit with engineers, understand what modern AI — LLMs and agents — can and cannot do, and still talk in the language of customers and unit economics.

After graduation I joined Vinay Enterprises as Operations Manager. For about twenty months I handled the business end-to-end: day-to-day logistics, inventory, and quality across 500-plus SKUs, and kept stock discrepancy under 2%. I built Excel dashboards that cut reporting time by 40%, led a team of eight with weekly KPIs, and lifted on-time completion by 25%. I also took a 0-to-1 bet — I designed and launched the company’s live web product so we could reach customers beyond offline operations.

That mix is why this role fits. I’mbesideyou is not asking for slide decks. You want a consultant who will find an AI-native opportunity, pair with an engineer, launch an MVP, and own customers, pricing, and first revenue. I have done the operations version of that ownership. I want to do it at AI scale — from hypothesis to paying accounts.

## Delivery notes

- **Start with the role, not the family.** Family is the value line, not the story. If you spend 30 seconds on home, you sound like every other intro and you miss the JD.
- **Numbers are the resume speaking.** 500+ SKUs, <2%, 40%, 8 people, 25%, 9.34 CGPA. Do not drop them.
- **Never say “end-to-end something.”** Say the three words: logistics, inventory, quality.
- **Do not fake Japanese.** If asked: “I have not worked in Japan yet. I am interested in how Japanese enterprises run operations, which is why ProcMine and this role stood out. I am ready to work across the Japan–India team on Slack and daily reports.”
- **If asked “Why not a product/engineering role?”** “I can build. I want to own the business outcome — customers, pricing, and whether it makes money — while my engineer pair owns the product.”
- **If asked “What is an AI-native business?”** “AI is the product, not a feature. Example: an agent that runs a store process, a caller that closes a booking, a process-mining layer that turns tribal knowledge into a workflow you can improve. I would start with one painful, expensive process, talk to five customers, and only then define the MVP.”
- **If asked “Have you built with LLMs?”** Be honest: “I have a University of Washington Machine Learning specialization, a CS degree, and I am in MBA Business Analytics. I have not shipped a production LLM agent. What I do have is enough literacy to know when an LLM is the product versus a wrapper — and enough operations experience to start from the process and the customer, not the model.”
- **Close and stop.** Do not add hobbies, chess, or “I am a quick learner.” Silence after the last line is confidence.

## Practice checklist

1. Record yourself once. If you hear “like”, “basically”, “so yeah”, cut them.
2. Time it. If you are over 100 seconds, use the 60-second cut and expand only if they nod.
3. Say the Vinay numbers without looking down.
4. End on “hypothesis to paying accounts” / “first revenue” — that is their language.
