# Booze Shampoo — Go-To-Market Recommendation

**To:** Management, Rogue Ltd (also referred to as Rouge Ltd in the brief)  
**From:** Marketing Strategy  
**Subject:** Launch plan for Booze Shampoo under a lean budget

## Chosen option

**Option C.**

Go for a regional launch rather than a pan India launch in areas where the company has a reasonably strong distribution reach. Slowly improve the distribution in other markets and couple it with regional and BTL advertisement efforts. Once the product is successful in such test markets, launch a pan India campaign.

| | Option | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Immediately launch a pan India advertisement campaign as competitors might imitate the product. Slowly build up a strong distribution muscle to complement the campaign. | Reject |
| B | First build a strong distribution muscle, then go for a nationwide advertisement campaign. | Reject |
| **C** | **Regional launch in strong-distribution markets, regional + BTL ads, then pan-India once test markets work.** | **Choose this** |
| D | Defer the product launch for two years without any media or PR promotion. Once the company has requisite budgets, launch aggressively with a pan India campaign. | Reject |
| E | Same as C, plus set aside more budgets on PR activities. | Reject |

Launch regionally in South and West India, where distribution can already support demand. Back that launch with regional media and BTL. In parallel, add dealers and deepen reach in North, Central, and East India. After the product proves itself in those test markets *and* national distribution is in place, scale to a pan-India campaign.

Do **not** pick E. PR is already adequate. Extra PR spend would waste a lean budget.

## How this was decided without the later finance answers

The unanswered questions (last-year profit, industry average, exact launch wallet, loan capacity, last-year ad spend, ROI formula, current TVCs, competitor names, industry growth) were **not required** to pick among A–E. Those five options are strategy choices, not budget models. The answers already given were enough to kill four of them.

### Step 1 — Name what each option is actually testing

| Option | The real bet |
| --- | --- |
| A | Awareness first, availability later |
| B | Availability first *everywhere*, then awareness |
| C | Availability first *where it already exists*, then expand |
| D | Wait two years for money, then go national |
| E | Same as C, but spend more on PR |

This is a sequencing question: **ads vs distribution, national vs regional, now vs later, extra PR or not.**

### Step 2 — Keep only the facts that discriminate those bets

These answers were already in hand. Each one kills at least one option.

| Given fact | What it decides |

| PR network is already good. “No further effort on PR front are required.” | Kills **E**. Extra PR is the only difference between C and E, and the case already closed that door. |

| An expensive European import exists; Rogue will sell at ~1/10th that price. No ambassador planned. Product attributes + WOM are the plan. | Supports a regional test: the story can travel without a national ATL tax. No last-year P&L, ROI formula, or loan answer appears in that table. They were not load-bearing.

### Step 3 — Eliminate, then keep the last option standing

1. **Drop A.** The case already said ads without distribution are lethal, and named the regions that cannot support a national campaign. Imitation is not a license to waste 25 Cr.
2. **Drop D.** Two years of silence is not required by any given fact. The company can launch where it is already strong, with cheaper regional and BTL work. A decade-stale portfolio cannot wait.
3. **Drop E.** C and E are the same launch. E only adds PR money. The given answer is that PR needs no further effort.
4. **Drop B.** Distribution-before-ads is the right *principle*, but B applies it to the whole country. South and West are already ready. Building the remaining 1,500 dealers can happen *in parallel* with a regional launch, which is C, not B.
5. **Keep C.** It is the only option that uses the ready markets now, fixes the weak markets next, and holds national ATL until both gates are passed.

### Step 4 — What the missing finance answers would have been used for

They would size the *envelope*, not change the *choice*.

- Last-year profit, industry average, ad spend, and “budget available” would tell you **how large** the South/West media pocket can be, and how fast the 1,500 dealers can be added.
- “Can the company take a loan?” would tell you whether Phase 3 can be debt-funded. It would not justify A (national ads into weak markets) or D (a two-year freeze).
- ROI method would be how you *measure* the test markets after choosing C. It is not how you pick C.
- Competitor / imitation / foreign-entry answers would change **speed inside C** (faster dealer addition, tighter secrecy), not flip the answer to A.

If those later answers had shown a huge unused cash pile, C would still win over A: money does not fix empty shelves in the North. If they had shown almost no cash, C would still win over D: regional + BTL is the cheaper way to start, not a two-year blackout.

## Why this is the right call

The case is explicit: **advertising without distribution is lethal.** A national TV burst would create demand in North, Central, and East India, where the network is not ready. Stock-outs, wasted media, and a failed first impression would follow.

At the same time, waiting to build a full national network before any launch, or parking the product for two years, wastes a 10-year innovation gap, delays cash, and gives imitators a free window.

South and West are already “ready to take the sales challenge of a new product.” That is the cheapest, safest way to create the buzz and word-of-mouth the company wants, while the weaker regions are being fixed.

## What management should do

### Phase 1 — Test-market launch (South + West)

1. **Load the existing network first.** Prioritise chemists and large department stores already covered in South and West. Get pack, price, and visibility right before any media goes live in a city.
2. **Add general trade / pop outlets** in those same markets to pick up footfall the chemist channel misses.
3. **Advertise only where stock is live.** Use regional TV/radio/digital, in-store activation, sampling, and BTL — not a national ATL burst. The product’s beer-extract story is the hero; no brand ambassador is needed.
4. **Use the existing PR and event machine**, do not enlarge the PR budget. Seed the “beer conditioner” story through current contacts and sponsorships the company already runs.
5. **Price for India, protect mix.** Keep the ~1/10th-of-import price so the offer is accessible, but treat early buyers as a skim/trial pool, not a permanent discount war.
6. **Watch sell-out, repeat, and retailer feedback** for 1–2 quarters. This is the proof the board needs before a national spend.

### Phase 2 — Close the distribution gap (North, Central, East)

1. Onboard the **~1,500 dealers** flagged for North and Central; extend the same play into the East.
2. Appoint and train distributors before any regional burst in those states.
3. Expand into general trade only after primary distribution and secondary coverage are reliable.
4. Reserve the estimated **~Rs 10 Cr distribution** money for this work. It is a launch prerequisite, not an optional add-on.

### Phase 3 — National scale, only after two gates are passed

Scale to a pan-India campaign **only if**:

- South/West test markets show sell-through, repeat, and acceptable margins; and
- North/Central/East can actually fulfil advertised demand.

Then use XYZ Ltd for a national campaign (TV + activation + BTL). Keep endorsement off the plan unless test-market data says the product story is not carrying itself.

A full **Rs 25 Cr** national media plan should not be committed on day one. Fund regional media from a smaller envelope, then release the national budget against the two gates above.

## Why the other options fail

| Option | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| **A** — National ads first, distribution later | Ads without reach are lethal. Lean budget would be burned in North/Central/East, which cannot convert. Imitation risk is real, but empty shelves are worse. |
| **B** — Full national distribution first, then advertise | Right sequence in principle, but too slow and too binary. South and West can launch now. Waiting for every weak state delays learning, cash, and buzz. |
| **D** — Defer two years with no media or PR | Portfolio has been stale for a decade. The import exists; imitation risk is real. A two-year freeze donates the idea and the moment. |
| **E** — Same as C, plus extra PR budget | Same launch logic, wrong spend. PR is already sufficient; further PR money is discretionary waste. |

## Operating implications

- **Sales:** Higher order frequency, retailer education, and merchandising in South/West immediately; hiring/onboarding of ~1,500 dealers before those regions see ads.
- **Supply:** Outsourced production must lock capacity and quality for a regional spike first, then a national ramp. Do not advertise a national fill-rate the partner cannot hit.
- **Finance:** Do not treat Rs 35 Cr (25 + 10) as a single day-one cheque. Sequence distribution and regional media first; hold national ATL until the gates are met.
- **Risk:** Imitation is a reason to move in ready markets, not a reason to advertise nationally into empty shelves.

---

## Success measures

- **Distribution:** numeric and weighted distribution in South/West at launch; dealer additions vs the 1,500 target before any North/Central burst.
- **Commercial:** sell-out (not just sell-in), repeat rate, and contribution margin vs the skim target.
- **Brand:** unaided buzz / WOM in launched states, without a national media tax.
- **Readiness gate:** no national ATL until fulfilment in advertised states stays above an agreed service level.

---

## One-line brief for the board

Launch where you can sell, advertise only where you can stock, fix the rest of India in parallel, then go national. That is the only plan that respects both the product’s buzz potential and the company’s lean budget.
